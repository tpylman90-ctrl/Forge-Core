## Decision

**Do not promote.** The pointer is learning a source-selection signal above the wrong-source control, but is not reliably distinguishing who gave and who received the object. Changing the grammatical form can sharply change accuracy, and full paired correctness is rare.

This test is a **diagnostic** and not a new untouched qualification exam. It is internally authored, covers four hand-specified grammars and has only one frozen trained head. The wrong-source control is not a fair two-person binary-chance baseline; it was deliberately trained on incorrect source spans from a much larger candidate pool. Do not conclude that Forge has mastered semantic role binding or general language understanding.

## Next independent research gate

1. **Separate copying from semantics.** Measure entity-span detection independently of choosing the correct semantic role. Use a task-independent named-entity detector; do not leak generated gold names into feature selection.
2. **Compare explicit role-aware architectures.** A query-conditioned two-slot giver/recipient model versus the existing frozen pointer and a matched shuffled-label control, with equal training steps and public-language retention measurements.
3. **Make counterfactuals part of *training*, not the final test.** Use completely separate names, syntax families, verbs and seeds; the current diagnostic questions/seeds must stay out of optimizer updates.
4. **Challenge on externally authored natural-language instructions.** Independent annotation and multiple model seeds are required before any claim that role reasoning generalizes.
5. **Protect the original model.** Keep the audited 14k-step scratch-origin checkpoint and existing experimental heads unchanged. No pretrained weights merged, Android integration or personal data.

Report per-skill results and both-correct pair accuracy, not only training-set accuracy or single-case exact match. If stronger baselines still fail, prioritize data diversity, tokenization and general language modeling over head complexity.
