# Linguistic Why Lab — Controlled structural probes v0.1

**Purpose:** give Forge Core a way to test *why* a change in word order or form changes the interpretation of a sentence. This is an early research/evaluation lab, not a user-facing AI feature or evidence of comprehension.

## The three implemented diagnostic families

1. **Semantic roles:** swap the two nouns around a past-tense transitive verb. Both sentences remain grammatical, but who acts on whom is reversed.
2. **Agreement:** compare a third-person singular present verb with its bare stem in a controlled simple sentence. One follows standard written English third-person singular agreement; the other does not.
3. **Negation:** compare a past-tense affirmative event with its "did not + base form" denial. Both sentences are grammatical but their event polarities differ.

The labels include structured gold answers and short rule descriptions authored by us. None is supplied by a pretrained model or generated by the candidate neural network.

## Run

    python -m forge_core.learning.linguistic_why --out data/linguistic-probes
    python -m unittest tests/test_linguistic_why.py -v

The generator produces:

- \`train.jsonl\`: 15 original training examples with gold labels.
- \`eval-questions.jsonl\`: 15 evaluation questions WITHOUT gold answers.
- \`eval-gold.jsonl\`: held-out gold answers and rules, never passed to the model.
- \`manifest.json\`: dataset provenance, SHA256 hashes, split counts and limits.

Training and evaluation nouns are disjoint, but their **verb list and sentence templates overlap**. That makes the fixtures useful for testing code, NOT an independent generalization benchmark. A meaningful experiment must additionally test unseen verbs, constructions, naturally occurring language and different annotation sources.

## Scientific safeguards and next implementation

1. **Generate and verify labels** using deterministic programs, expert-reviewed grammar fixtures or adequately licensed annotations. Never allow the candidate model to self-approve its explanations.
2. **Build a task adapter** only after the next general-language baseline is measured. Structured classification or constrained role extraction may be more reliable than demanding long free-form explanations from an immature network.
3. **Control the comparison**: original scratch model and same-data, same-compute text-only controls versus a model given linguistic auxiliary labels. Compare multiple seeds.
4. **Hold out an external challenge set** with unseen vocabulary, unseen grammar constructions, independently sourced sentences and ambiguous cases. Report accuracy relative to chance and baselines, held-out language loss and generation-quality regressions.
5. **Expand gradually**: tense, pronoun/coreference, quantifiers, spatial and temporal event order, and causal claims. Avoid making unsupported leaps from correlation or chronological order to causation.
6. **Promote conditionally**: if the linguistic objective fails to transfer or damages general language, revise or remove it. We are not committed to this method regardless of outcomes.

This lab is **the first half** of our two different Why systems. The later personal Why loop requires a user's consent and confirmation and always writes to private, encrypted memory, not to this public fixture or neural weights by default.

## Neural A/B study (implemented, experimental)

The next milestone connects these rule-derived labels to the actual
scratch-trained Forge Seed checkpoint in a controlled experiment. See
[Linguistic Why A/B experiment](LINGUISTIC-WHY-EXPERIMENT.md).
Two independently initialized optimizer branches receive identical prompts
and schedules; only correct vs shuffled answer labels differ. A third frozen
branch measures the prior knowledge. Evaluators report held-out structural
answer accuracy and regression in next-byte language loss. This is not
self-training or personal reasoning, and **no trained candidate is promoted
without independent replication**.
