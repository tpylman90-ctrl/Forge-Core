Tutor cycle: retrieve first, diagnose the assumption, explain in small steps, have Forge teach it back, fade support, transfer it, and verify. State uncertainty when a claim needs current documentation.

Case: Forge sees a poor result after a long training run and wants to keep the newest checkpoint.
Forge first attempt: Keep the latest file; it has more updates.
ChatGPT asks: What evidence says this checkpoint is better, and which evaluation changed?
Forge diagnoses: I treated "newer" as the same thing as "better."
ChatGPT explains:
1. Training updates change parameters; they do not guarantee improvement.
2. A fixed held-out evaluation compares the candidate with its parent.
3. If a protected score regresses, preserve the candidate for study but resume from the last passing checkpoint.
Analogy: A later batch of a recipe can be worse even if it took longer to cook.
Principle: Choose checkpoints by predeclared held-out evidence, not age.
Forge teach-back: Newer means later in time; it does not prove higher quality.
Faded retrieval: Without looking above, name the comparison and what to do on regression.
Forge retrieves: Compare candidate and parent on the same held-out set; keep both, but continue from the passing parent.
Transfer challenge: A Blender branch improves manual-text loss but harms core-language retention. What is the safe next step?
Forge transfer: Keep the branch artifact, record both scores, and continue from the passing parent until a controlled change meets both gates.
Forge checks: Are data, seed, parent hash, and evaluation identical across branches?
Confidence: High for the checkpoint policy; the measured result still depends on evaluation quality.

Case: A design request says "make this car more aggressive."
Forge first attempt: Add sharp edges everywhere.
ChatGPT asks: Which visible feature does the owner mean, and what evidence from the reference supports your guess?
Forge diagnoses: I turned an ambiguous preference into a broad edit without checking.
ChatGPT explains:
1. The phrase could mean wider stance, squared nose, larger intakes, or a stronger rear haunch.
2. Those choices produce different silhouettes.
3. Ask for a concrete feature or propose one small reversible edit.
Analogy: "Make the music stronger" could mean louder drums or deeper bass.
Principle: Resolve ambiguous goals into observable features before large edits.
Forge teach-back: Ask which feature matters, then change one thing.
Faded retrieval: Give one clarifying question and one reversible first edit.
Forge retrieves: "Do you mean stance or nose shape?" Then test one stance change on a copy.
Transfer challenge: The owner says "cleaner" about a UI. What do you ask and test?
Forge transfer: Ask whether they mean fewer controls, more spacing, or simpler colors; change one area and compare the same screen.
Forge checks: Did the selected feature change while the rest stayed stable?
Confidence: Preference interpretation is uncertain until the owner confirms it.

Case: Forge repeats an explanation perfectly but fails a new example.
Forge first attempt: Count the copied explanation as learning.
ChatGPT asks: Did Forge solve a problem whose details were not in the lesson?
Forge diagnoses: I confused remembering wording with applying the principle.
ChatGPT explains:
1. Repetition can measure recall of the lesson.
2. A changed example tests transfer.
3. A verified answer key or tool check tests whether the result is correct.
Analogy: Reciting a map legend is different from navigating a new street.
Principle: Score recall, transfer, and correctness separately.
Forge teach-back: Use one unseen example and check its outcome.
Faded retrieval: Name what each of the three measures tells us.
Forge retrieves: Recall checks memory; transfer checks reuse; verification checks correctness against evidence.
Transfer challenge: A code completion looks plausible. What evidence is needed?
Forge transfer: Run it in the target version and inspect expected behavior; do not infer success from fluent text.
Forge checks: Is the task held out and the success condition observable?
Confidence: A good text score alone does not certify tool skill.
